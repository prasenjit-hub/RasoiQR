# RasoiQR — Go-to-Market Strategy

> **Market:** Dharmanagar, North Tripura · **Goal:** 10 paying restaurants in 90 days · **Founder:** solo developer based in Dharmanagar; no field visits; WhatsApp, Facebook, and online outreach only.
>
> **Planning assumptions:** Population (~50k), segments, conversion rates, channels, incentives, and growth goals below are supplied planning inputs or hypotheses—not independently verified market research. Track actual results weekly and revise. Product capabilities and list pricing are checked against the current app; proposed discounts need an explicit founder decision and should not be advertised until approved.

## Executive Playbook

Win a small, well-defined local niche by selling **a simpler direct-ordering and menu workflow**, not “another food-delivery app.” Start with restaurants that already have a Google listing and enough table turnover to benefit from QR menus. Lead with a short, personalized WhatsApp conversation, show a two-minute demo, then offer a guided 14-day trial with menu setup. Measure whether customers actually scan and whether owners continue using the order dashboard. Convert on observed value—not fear or unsupported promises.

**90-day target funnel (working hypothesis):** 10 paying accounts. Start with 50 qualified leads, then replenish weekly. At 1–2% paid close per unique cold contact, the raw math is 500–1,000 contacts; referrals and local content may reduce cold volume, but do not count them until measured. A solo founder should test a sustainable cadence and review weekly rather than blindly chasing 1,000 names. It is not yet verified that Dharmanagar has 500–1,000 qualified prospects; count the real lead universe in week one. If it is smaller, this target depends on unusually strong referrals/conversion or carefully approved outreach into nearby areas. The 5–10% reply and 1–2% close rates are supplied assumptions, not local research or guarantees. “Paying restaurant” means first payment received—not a trial, verbal commitment, or unpaid invoice.

**Positioning in one sentence:** “RasoiQR helps local restaurants publish a phone-friendly menu and receive direct dine-in/takeaway orders for a predictable monthly price, without app downloads or per-order platform commission.” Be precise: RasoiQR does not currently process online payments, automate subscription billing, guarantee more customers, or replace every waiter/POS workflow.

## 1. Target Customer Profile

| Attribute | Initial target |
|---|---|
| Restaurant | 20–80 seats; local cuisine; family-run or owner-managed |
| Decision-maker | Owner or family member who controls menu and daily operations |
| Owner profile | Approx. 30–55, uses WhatsApp daily, moderate tech comfort (hypothesis to validate) |
| Digital readiness | Existing Google Business Profile, Facebook/Instagram page, or customers who already use smartphone QR payments |
| Pain points to validate | Waiter dependency during rush; order mistakes; stale/printed menus; no easy shareable menu link; concern about marketplace commissions |
| Buying triggers | Festival/holiday rush, a new competitor, renovation/opening, menu/pricing change, expansion, or a recent service bottleneck |
| Disqualifiers | Very low table volume, no reliable phone/internet access for staff, owner unwilling to update menu, or expectation of delivery marketplace/payment/POS features RasoiQR does not provide |

Do not assume every restaurant wants customers to order at the table. Ask how orders are taken today, where mistakes happen, and whether a QR menu fits their service style. A QR menu can complement staff; do not claim it eliminates waiter jobs or guarantees labor savings.

## 2. Customer Segments (Priority Order)

1. **Tier 1 — Mid-size restaurants (30–50 seats) with existing Google presence.** Best first fit: sufficient order volume, identifiable decision-maker, and some digital discoverability. Start here.
2. **Tier 2 — Small cafés and tea stalls with regular customers.** Test a master QR and simple takeaway/parcel use. Confirm their average order value and whether QR changes service enough to justify the subscription.
3. **Tier 3 — New restaurants opening soon.** Approach before opening so menu and QR material can be prepared. Be clear that launch-day reliability should be tested before relying on it.
4. **Tier 4 — Cloud kitchens.** Potential fit for a shareable direct menu, but delivery management and online payment are not currently product capabilities; qualify carefully and avoid selling those workflows as built.

**Lead score (0–5):** +1 if 30+ seats; +1 active Google/Facebook presence; +1 owner contact found; +1 visible menu/order friction; +1 opening/expansion/festival trigger. Prioritize score 4–5, then learn from 3s. Disqualify if they need unsupported integrations as a condition of purchase.

### A) WhatsApp Outreach — Primary

**Finding contacts:** Use public business contact details from Google Maps/Business Profiles, restaurant Facebook pages, and Justdial listings where available. Respect platform terms, privacy expectations, and applicable anti-spam/telemarketing rules. Do not scrape or bulk-blast lists. Research one lead at a time, message business contact channels only, identify yourself honestly, and stop after a clear “no” or opt-out. Never buy private-number lists.

**Message template (Hinglish; personalize every time):**

> Namaste [Owner Name] ji, main [Founder Name], Dharmanagar se. [Restaurant Name] ka [specific menu/dine-in detail] dekha—achha laga. **Expected conversion:** Use the requested 5–10% reply and 1–2% paid-close rates only as hypotheses. If replies are good but trials do not start, improve discovery/demo; if trials start but do not pay, interview owners about setup effort, missing capabilities, value, and price before increasing volume.

### C) Local Influencers — Tertiary

Identify small Tripura food vloggers whose audience is local and whose content gets real comments, not only follower counts. Ask for audience/location insights and typical deliverables. Offer a **clearly disclosed pilot/barter**—for example, a free account for a defined period or a lifetime account only if the founder approves the long-term cost. Do not require a positive review; request an honest demo/review and disclose the exchange. Provide a unique link/QR to attribute visits. Do not promise influencer reach or sales.

### D) Offline Referrals — Compound (Remote-Friendly)

Although the founder cannot do field visits, referrals can be requested and fulfilled remotely over WhatsApp. After a restaurant has used RasoiQR successfully for at least one week, ask the owner to introduce one or two peers in a WhatsApp group or forward a short intro. Proposed incentive: one month free per referred restaurant after that referral becomes a paying customer. Track referrer, referral date, conversion date, credit issued, and expiry; cap/define stacking before launch. Do not issue credits for unqualified leads or self-referrals.

### E) QR Sticker Distribution — Indirect

Do **not** print 500 stickers before testing cost, scan behavior, and placement. Start with 25–50 durable stickers/mini-cards for consenting pilot restaurants or partner businesses. Use a campaign-specific destination/UTM link and clear branding. Avoid placing stickers without permission or in a way that suggests a venue endorses RasoiQR. Expand toward 500 only if scan-to-qualified-lead cost is competitive and attribution works.

## 4. Sales Process (5 Steps)

1. **Discovery (WhatsApp question):** Ask one easy question, not a feature checklist: “Rush time me order kaise lete hain, aur sabse common confusion kya hota hai?” Then ask what they would want a QR menu to improve. Qualify table count, ordering style, current digital menu, decision-maker, and timing.
2. **Demo (2-minute video + optional live call):** Show: scan master/table QR → browse/search → customize → place a sample order → owner sees it in the order dashboard. Use a test restaurant, label the demo, and avoid using real customer data. Offer a 10–15 minute screen-share for questions; no pressure.
3. **Trial setup (guided menu configuration):** Start the documented 14-day trial. Agree the start date and trial end date in writing. Help import/enter an initial menu, verify prices/photos/availability, generate a small set of table QRs, and place a test order. Explain supported dine-in/takeaway flow and unsupported payment/delivery features.
4. **Trial follow-up (Day 7 check-in):** Ask whether the menu is live, whether customers scanned, what staff found confusing, and whether any order was missed. Review simple usage evidence with owner permission. Offer remote setup help; do not imply orders were generated if they were not.
5. **Conversion (trial → paid):** Around Day 11–12, recap measured value and plan options; send exact price, billing period, taxes if applicable, and payment instructions. Ask for a decision before the end date. Handle objections honestly. Since automated recurring billing/payment is not integrated, document the manual payment and renewal process before taking money; send receipts/invoices as legally/accountingly appropriate.

**Trial close message (standard pricing):**

> [Name] ji, aapka 14-day trial [date] ko khatam hoga. Is dauraan [verified usage/result—e.g. menu live, QR scanned/test order count] hua. Agar continue karna ho, Starter ₹499/month ya Pro ₹999/month hai. RasoiQR abhi online payment checkout/auto-renewal nahi karta; payment [approved method/process] se manually confirm hoga. Kya main plan details bhejun, ya aap trial ke baad pause karna chahenge?

**Founding-five variant (only after offer approval):**

> Aap founding-five setup offer ke liye qualify karte hain: Starter ₹399/month pehle 3 paid months ke liye, phir ₹499/month. Pro ₹999/month rahega. Trial 14 din ka hi hai. Offer [expiry date] ko band hoga; koi auto-renewal nahi. Payment [approved method/process] se manually hoga. Kya main terms aur setup slot bhejun?

## 5. Objection Handling

Keep responses conversational, ask one follow-up question, and never use invented customer adoption statistics. The figures below are examples; replace with verified restaurant-specific numbers.

### “Bahut mehnga hai”

> Samajh sakta hoon, har monthly kharcha justify hona chahiye. Starter ₹499/month hai—lagbhag ₹17/day. Yeh waiter ko replace karne ka promise nahi hai; QR menu menu-sharing aur order entry ko simpler kar sakta hai. Agar isse aapke staff ka [measured minutes] bachta hai ya menu reprint kam hota hai, tab value banti hai. Pehle trial me measure karte hain; agar fit na ho to continue mat kijiye.

Do not compare ₹499 to an ₹8,000 waiter salary as if one replaces the other. A ₹8,000 salary example may be shown only as a transparent illustration of relative cost—not a guaranteed saving or job replacement. Verify local wage figures before using them in marketing.

### “Zomato ka commission kam hai”

> Ho sakta hai aapke current contract me commission aapko theek lagta ho. RasoiQR marketplace nahi hai: QR se aane wale direct orders par hum per-order commission nahi lete; monthly software fee hai. Yeh Zomato/Swiggy ka replacement nahi—unke discovery/delivery reach alag hai. Aap apne actual monthly commission aur order source se compare karke decide kijiye.

### “Mere customers QR nahi scan karenge”

> Yeh genuine concern hai; main adoption ka unsupported percentage claim nahi karunga. Aap QR ke saath printed menu/staff assistance rakh sakte hain. Trial me 1–2 tables par test karke scans aur completed orders count karte hain. Agar customers use na karein, aapko evidence mil jayega bina poore restaurant ko badle.

Use only observed pilot numbers with consent and context; do not say “most customers scan” unless measured and defensible.

### “Menu banane ka time nahi hai”

> Bilkul, setup me time lagta hai. Main aapke existing menu/photo/list se initial items remotely enter karne me help kar sakta hoon—pehle [agreed number] items. Aap prices, spelling, veg/non-veg aur options verify karenge before QR goes live. Done-for-you setup ka exact scope/time pehle agree karenge; menu changes baad me owner dashboard se honge.

Do not promise unlimited data entry or accept a menu as correct without owner review.

### “Baad mein dekhta hoon”

> Zaroor. Kya main [specific date] ko ek baar follow-up karun, ya aap khud message karenge? Agar abhi festival/launch ke liye relevant nahi, koi urgency nahi. [If approved: “First five Dharmanagar pilot restaurants ke liye menu setup launch offer [real expiry date] tak hai.”] Main false scarcity create nahi karunga.

**Urgency rule:** A limited-time discount is valid only if it has a real cap, published terms, and a real end date. Do not invent “only 2 spots left.” If no approved offer exists, omit the scarcity line.

### Recommended launch offer — research-informed proposal, approval required

**Recommended “founding five” offer:** Keep the public list prices (Starter ₹499/month, Pro ₹999/month). For the first **five qualified Dharmanagar restaurants** that begin a trial within an actual 30-day launch window, offer Starter at **₹399/month for their first three paid months**, then ₹499/month. Keep Pro at ₹999 unless customer interviews show a Pro-specific price barrier. The offer saves a Starter restaurant ₹100/month, **₹300 total over three paid months** (20% off for a defined period), not 50% off for six months. It stays above the proposed ₹299 floor and sits near an observed public competitor entry price.

Pair the offer with bounded founder onboarding: one remote session (up to 30 minutes) and menu setup for up to 25 items; the owner must verify every item/price before publishing. No physical print materials, photo editing, unlimited revisions, or promise of a positive testimonial. Ask for structured product feedback, but make public reviews optional. The offer requires a real expiry date, written terms, and founder approval before advertising; do not stack it with annual or referral discounts.

**Why this is the better first test:** Public pricing suggests ₹499 is not obviously out of market, but competitors range from free/simple menus to ₹399 monthly direct-ordering suites. Some lower-priced competitors advertise more capabilities (payments, delivery, loyalty) than RasoiQR currently has, so RasoiQR should not compete on feature breadth or claim ₹499 is the cheapest. The recommended offer tests two plausible conversion frictions—price and menu setup—while protecting most of the list price and giving the founder usable onboarding feedback. The discount cost is bounded: if all five buy Starter for all three paid months, the maximum list-price concession is **5 × ₹100 × 3 = ₹1,500**, plus the founder’s capped setup time.

**Do not lead with a deep discount.** A 50% Starter discount is ₹249.50/month, below the ₹299 floor; over six paid months it is ₹1,497 instead of ₹2,994, a ₹1,497 concession per account (up to ₹14,970 for ten Starter accounts). It risks establishing a low reference price before willingness-to-pay is understood. Trial and discount solve different problems: use the existing 14-day trial to prove the product; use the small ₹399 offer only to test verified price sensitivity. If setup is the main objection, keep full price and offer the bounded setup instead.

**Market check (public advertised prices; not an apples-to-apples survey):** [AhaarScan](https://www.ahaarscan.com/terms-and-conditions) lists ₹399/month and ₹2,999/year; it advertises a broader direct-ordering suite, so its exact scope should be checked before comparison. [Petpooja’s QR-ordering guide](https://blog.petpooja.com/industry-business-guides/choose-qr-ordering-system-restaurant/) describes standalone tools as lower-cost, with some under ₹500/month, and warns about manual POS reconciliation. [TryTheMenu](https://www.trythemenu.com/) lists pay-per-closed-bill pricing (2 credits = ₹2 + GST) with no monthly subscription. Prices and offers can change; public vendor claims are not proof of Dharmanagar willingness-to-pay. Confirm current prices directly before sales comparisons. Local lead count and customer price sensitivity still need field evidence via remote interviews.

**Annual pricing:** UI currently lists Starter ₹4,999/year (12 × ₹499 = ₹5,988, ₹989 / ~16.5% less) and Pro ₹9,999/year (12 × ₹999 = ₹11,988, ₹1,989 / ~16.6% less). These already provide an annual prepayment discount. Initially lead with the 14-day trial and monthly plan; offer annual only after an owner has experienced the product. Do not stack annual pricing with the founding-five offer.

**Referral incentive:** Keep one month free per referred restaurant as a later test, payable only after the referral’s first paid month. Define credit expiry/stacking and do not layer it on the launch offer.

**Price floor:** Keep ₹299/month as the hard experiment floor unless the founder explicitly changes it. The recommended ₹399 offer is above it; do not advertise any promotion until founder approval, end date, renewal price, payment process, and manual billing tracking are ready.

## 8. Metrics to Track

Use a simple spreadsheet/CRM at first; one row per restaurant, one row per outreach touch. Record date, source, segment, lead score, status, next step, opt-out, and reason lost. Avoid storing sensitive customer order data in the sales sheet.

| Funnel | Metric | Definition / cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Outreach | Messages sent | Unique, personalized first contacts; exclude follow-ups. Weekly by channel/segment. |
| Outreach | Reply rate | Human replies ÷ first contacts delivered. Report positive/neutral/negative separately. |
| Outreach | Demo bookings/show rate | Demos booked and attended ÷ replies or qualified leads; define denominator consistently. |
| Trials | Trial activated | Restaurant with owner profile + menu + at least one QR tested; do not count a signup alone. |
| Trials | Trial active | Has opened owner dashboard or received/tested order in the last 7 days; define tracking method and disclose if manually observed. |
| Trials | Trial-to-paid conversion | First paid accounts ÷ trials whose 14-day period ended. Cohort by start week. |
| Revenue | MRR | Sum of active monthly-equivalent subscription revenue; exclude trials and one-time setup. Apply discounts and tax treatment consistently. |
| Revenue | ARPU | MRR ÷ active paying restaurants. Report plan mix and discounted/full-price cohorts. |
| Revenue | Churn | Paying accounts lost in period ÷ paying accounts at period start; define voluntary/involuntary cancellation consistently. With 10 accounts, show counts alongside percentages. |
| Product | Orders/day per restaurant | Valid orders ÷ active service days; exclude test/rolled-back orders and clearly define date range. |
| Product | QR activation | Restaurants with a QR scanned/tested in venue ÷ trials activated. Track aggregate counts only where possible. |
| Quality | Setup time / support minutes | Founder time per onboarding/account per week; vital for a solo founder and done-for-you offer economics. |

**Weekly dashboard (minimum):** 90-day goal progress (paid accounts), qualified leads, replies, attended demos, active trials, converted trials, MRR, churned accounts, orders/day per restaurant, founder support time, top three objections, and opt-outs. Review every Sunday; change one experiment at a time.

## 9. Content Marketing (Long-Term)

Keep content useful for local restaurant operators and repurpose each demo into short formats. A solo founder should aim for a sustainable cadence (e.g., one useful video/article and two short clips per week), not daily content at the expense of onboarding.

- **YouTube — “Restaurant business tips in Tripura”:** Short, practical videos about menu photos, peak-hour ordering, QR placement, Google listing hygiene, and owner interviews (with consent). Show the product honestly; label demo data and disclose any free-account exchange.
- **Blog — SEO for “restaurant menu QR India”:** Create durable guides: QR menu setup, table vs master QR, menu photo checklist, local-language menu considerations, and transparent cost comparisons. Avoid claims that imply Google rankings, guaranteed order growth, or competitor performance without evidence.
- **Instagram/Reels:** 20–45 second screen captures showing actual product flows: scan → customize → order appears in dashboard; QR standee print; sold-out item toggle. Use captions/subtitles, low-data exports, and real permissions for restaurant footage.
- **Distribution:** Post to the restaurant’s own channels only with owner permission. Use a trackable link and record qualified inquiries attributed to each post.

## 10. What Not to Do

- **Do not chase restaurants with fewer than 10 tables** as the default ICP. Exception only if the owner has a clear use case and the economics make sense.
- **Do not routinely extend beyond the 14-day free trial.** If a technical incident prevented a fair trial, document the reason and a specific correction window rather than granting open-ended free access.
- **Do not drop price below ₹299/month** without explicit founder approval and a unit-economics review.
- **Do not cold-call.** Use WhatsApp first as requested; only call if the owner asks or gives clear permission.
- **Do not promise features that do not exist:** online payments, POS integration, delivery management, customer adoption rates, custom domains, automatic billing, or guaranteed revenue/labor savings.
- Do not send mass unsolicited blasts, scrape private numbers, ignore opt-outs, post repeated group ads, or misrepresent a barter review as independent.
- Do not quote unsupported local wage, commission, market-size, or customer adoption statistics as facts.
- Do not print or place QR stickers without permission; avoid spending on 500 units before a small attribution test.
- Do not count a signup, trial, demo, unpaid promise, or influencer post as one of the 10 paying restaurants.

## 11. Expansion Triggers (When to Move to Agartala)

Treat each threshold as a **gate to validate**, not a guarantee of product-market fit. Move into active Agartala acquisition only when all core gates are met and support capacity is sustainable:

- **10 paying customers in Dharmanagar:** First payment received and account active; exclude free/barter accounts unless they later pay.
- **500+ genuine orders processed:** Exclude tests, duplicates, and rolled-back verification activity; verify against production order records and agree what “processed” means.
- **Churn below 10%:** Measure over a rolling period (recommend trailing 90 days), disclose denominator and absolute lost-account count. With a small base, one cancellation moves the percentage sharply; interpret cautiously.
- **Positive word-of-mouth referrals:** At least three qualified referrals from existing paying owners is a practical initial signal; record source and whether they convert.
- **Operational readiness:** Onboarding checklist works remotely, median setup/support time is manageable, backups/restore are verified, and trial-to-paid/payment handling is documented.

After the Dharmanagar gates are met, begin a small remote acquisition test in Agartala; add Kailashahar after the onboarding/support process remains sustainable across the first expansion cohort. Reuse the same qualified-lead, reply, activation, paid-conversion, and churn measures rather than assuming another city behaves identically.

If the 90-day target is missed, do not automatically expand geography. Interview lost leads and trial users, find the bottleneck (fit, trust, activation, product gaps, pricing, or payment process), run one focused Dharmanagar experiment, then reassess.
